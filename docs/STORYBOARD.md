# Storyboard — Every Great Idea Starts With a Scribble

## Story summary

A group of colorful stick figures explores five ways to contribute to ACM-VIT. Tech builds working tools; Research investigates questions; Management brings people and plans together; Design shapes visual experiences; and CC (Competitive Coding) tackles algorithms, data structures, and timed challenges. The final scene reunites all five figures. Readers can enter any chapter directly.

## Character and creature references

**Protagonist:** A university-age student with a recognizable side-swept, slightly messy hair silhouette, round glasses, rolled sleeves, and a pencil tucked beside the notebook. Keep head, hair, glasses, and jacket shapes consistent; vary only pose and expression. Draw in layered graphite contours and cross-hatching.

**Creature:** A small round-bodied mechanical bird named Scrib. Preserve its circular head, one oversized eye, short beak, two spring legs, and three feather-like antennae throughout. Add construction-line body first, then circuit core, sensors, team-built frame, designed markings, and finally a small blue/red accent. Never change its silhouette beyond those readable additions.

## Spread sequence

1. **Cover — First scribble:** The student sits beside an oversized pencil, drawing the incomplete creature. One eye and a few construction circles are dark; the rest is faint. Note: “WHAT IF...?”
2. **Discover — Five paths:** The protagonist stands where five pencil pathways branch into circuit traces, an experiment graph, a team timeline, shape studies, and speech-bubble links. Domain index and compact descriptions share this spread.
3. **Tech — Give it a brain:** At a cluttered workbench, Scrib gains a circuit core and joints. A small two-panel gag shows it stumble, then the protagonist debug. “IT WORKS... MOSTLY.”
4. **Research — Ask why:** Scrib is observed through diagrams, a hypothesis, an experiment graph, crossed-out assumptions, and revised notes. “BUT WHY DOES IT WORK?”
5. **Management — Make it possible:** The overwhelmed protagonist is joined by teammates. Scattered parts resolve into a plan, board, and shared assembly. “WE NEED A PLAN.”
6. **Design — Give it an identity:** Shape studies and interface sketches refine the working creature. Restrained pencil-blue and accent-red marks appear. “WHAT IF IT FELT ALIVE?”
7. **CC — Think. Solve. Optimize.:** A green stick figure celebrates with a pink teammate beside an algorithm board marked “O(n)” and “ACCEPTED.” The speech bubble reads “Accepted! On to the next problem.” Application prompts ask about coding platforms or contests and a problem-solving approach.
8. **Final — Bigger picture:** The entire team and completed Scrib gather in a double-page scene. Circuits, research marks, plans, design forms, and conversations converge. “EVERY GREAT IDEA STARTS WITH A SCRIBBLE. THE NEXT ONE COULD BE YOURS. JOIN THE STORY.”

## Visual progression and continuity

Use a warm paper ground, graphite `#242320`, lighter construction marks, and only occasional pencil blue `#526D84` and accent red `#A74C43`. Contours darken and hatch density grows from cover to CC; the final spread is the most complete. A loose pencil line exits each chapter's illustration and resumes on the next scene. Repeat the creature's eye in corner doodles and carry a tiny part, diagram, schedule mark, silhouette study, and poster into successive chapters. These illustrations are generated from a shared character/creature drawing system on page textures, not independent stock assets.
